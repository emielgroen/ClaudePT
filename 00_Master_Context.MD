# 00 Master Context & Medical History

## Core Goal

> **To become completely pain-free in daily life, and systematically improve knee capacity to eventually remain pain-free even when exercising as much as desired.**

---

## Surgical & Clinical Background

- **Left Knee:**
    - History of habitual patellar dislocation due to a laterally positioned tibial tuberosity.
    - **2015-11:** Last full dislocation (treated conservatively).
    - **2016-09-07:** Tibial Tuberosity Transfer (TTT / medialization) performed by Dr. Zurcher at Diakonessenhuis Utrecht.
    - **Post-Op:** One subsequent dislocation leading to arthroscopy to remove a loose bone fragment. Kneecap assessed as structurally sound afterward.
    - **2016–2025:** Largely symptom-free.
- **Right Knee:**
    - Unoperated, but shares history of lateralized tracking/pains.
    - Currently experiences **higher symptom intensity** than the left knee, likely due to right-side overcompensation.

---

## Symptom Profile & Mechanics

- **Sensation Type:**
    - Primary feeling is **pins and needles / tingling / buzzing** rather than sharp structural locking.
    - Centered at the front and sides of the kneecap, radiating into upper legs (quads) and calves.
    - Accompanied by **restless legs at night**, severely impacting sleep.
- **Severity & Behavior:**
    - Rated up to **5/10** at worst.
    - Constant baseline "tingle"; acute pain triggered by knee bending, stairs, running, or long walks.
    - Occasional minor visible swelling, clicking, and feeling of the knee "giving way."
    - Secondary lower back stiffness due to gait compensation.
- **Trigger Patterns & Activity Tolerances:**
    - **High Risk / Triggers:**
        - Running
        - Padel
        - Walking through cities for too long
        - Standing for too long
    - **Conditional / Capacity-Dependent:**
        - **Cycling (Racebike):** Fine if not pushed too hard or if general fatigue is low. Triggers pain if volume/intensity is high or if performed after other heavy workouts.
        - **Rucking (6kg backpack):** Generally fine, but will aggravate symptoms if the knees are already in an active flare-up.
    - **High Tolerance / Active Recovery:**
        - Dynamic, multi-planar activities with varied joint angles and soft surfaces (3-day trail hiking with 1000m elevation, snowboarding, surfing).
        - **Yoga** (completely fine)
        - **Supping (Stand Up Paddleboarding)** (completely fine)
    - **Delayed Onset:** Symptoms rarely peak on Day 1 or 2; full flare-ups typically emerge on **Day 3** post-activity (Day 3 Latency Window).

## Differential Diagnostic Framework & Potential Mechanisms

*(Note: These are potential clinical mechanisms to guide tracking and discuss with your physical therapist, not fixed diagnoses.)*

1. **Patellofemoral Pain Syndrome (PFPS) & Tracking Imbalance (Most Common):**
   - **Mechanism:** Imbalance between inner/outer quadriceps (VMO vs. lateral quad/IT band) and hip stabilizers causing altered kneecap glide along the femoral groove.
   - **Clinical Fit:** Pain, soreness, or tingling centered around and below the kneecap; clicking on extension; aggravation with prolonged sitting, stairs, running, or squats. Fits both knees.

2. **Postural Hyperextension & Asymmetrical Loading (Biomechanical):**
   - **Mechanism:** Standing idle with locked-out knees disengages active muscle support and shifts body weight onto passive joint structures. Overcompensating onto the right unoperated leg increases right-side wear and tear.
   - **Clinical Fit:** Right knee symptom intensity higher after long standing; noticeable relief when actively maintaining "soft knees."

3. **Infrapatellar Soft Tissue & Fat Pad Sensitization (Anterior Compression):**
   - **Mechanism:** Mechanical compression or inflammation of the infrapatellar structures (Hoffa's fat pad or patellar tendon) situated directly below the kneecap, especially during repetitive heel strikes on flat ground or full extension.
   - **Clinical Fit:** Symptoms localized specifically in front of the knee just below the patella; high sensitivity to hard pavement walking and prolonged standing.

4. **Superficial Cutaneous Nerve & Scar Sensitivity (Left Knee Post-TTT):**
   - **Mechanism:** Surgical incision from the 2016 TTT can cause minor scar tethering or sensitivity of local sensory nerve branches (e.g., infrapatellar saphenous nerve).
   - **Clinical Fit:** Superficial tingling, buzzing, or pins-and-needles across the front and sides of the left knee scar under repetitive motion.

## Effective Relief Factors

- **Magnesium Supplements:** Significantly reduces night restlessness and muscle/nerve twitching.
- **Painkillers:** Mild reduction in baseline ache.
- **Isometric Loading & Soft Surfaces:** Reduces active fat pad compression.
