# Independent Home Knee Program (blind design)

Basis: 2018 International PFP consensus/best-practice guidance (combined hip + knee exercise, education, load management; taping/orthoses only as short-term adjuncts; no passive-only care), the pain-monitoring model (Silbernagel/Thomeé), and general patellar-instability rehab principles. Built from the brief only.

---

## 1. Clinical reasoning (priorities)

1. **The symptom quality doesn't look purely like PFPS.** Tingling, buzzing and pins-and-needles that spread to the quads and calves, plus restless legs at night that ease with magnesium, suggest a nerve component (nerve sensitivity, possibly coming from the lower back given the back stiffness) as well as a joint load problem. I'd ask a GP/physio for a one-off check: a lumbar/neural screen (slump test, femoral nerve tension, dermatomes) and bloods (ferritin, B12, vitamin D, since low iron is a common restless-legs driver). The program below stays the same either way, but it includes gentle nerve mobility work and an easy cue: if a symptom moves around rather than staying put, that is not a reason to stop.
2. **The delayed 48–72 h response is the main thing to design around.** Rule: no two "knee-loaded" days back to back (strength session, padel, a hike over 8 km, a hard ride, a long city day). Score each session on day 1, 2 and 3, not just straight after.
3. **The hip-to-quad balance is the wrong way round.** The quads have been getting most of the work while the right glute med tires early. Every session opens with hip work, and hip volume is about equal to quad volume.
4. **The quads still need capacity, but through tolerable range and tempo.** Use isometrics and slow eccentrics in mid-range (roughly 0–60° flexion) first, then deeper range. Wedge/slant-board work is a strong stimulus that gets dosed like a heavy lift, not used as a finisher.
5. **Left-leg control (post-op quad inhibition) and the right side's overload are different problems.** Single-leg quad and balance work starts with the left leg. Hip abductor and rotator work starts with the right. The other side matches the reps; it never does more.
6. **Hyperextension and locked-knee idling feed the right-knee overload and fat pad irritation.** Use "soft-straight" knees (about 5° of bend) in daily life and in training. I would not use hard lockout squeezes as a regular feature.
7. **Relaxed, varied movement is tolerated better than guarded, braced movement.** Coach confident, fluid movement. Never cue "brace your knee."
8. **Change one thing at a time.** Progress one variable in one exercise per session, and keep total weekly volume increases around 10–15%.
9. **Padel needs specific prep.** That means lateral deceleration, split-step, lunging to a stop, and jumping and landing on hard courts, built up as a graded exposure ladder before match play.
10. **Ramp re-entry after layoffs.** After a layoff of 7 days or more, restart at about 60% of the last volume and rebuild over 1–2 weeks.

## 2. Weekly structure

| Day | Content |
|---|---|
| Mon | **Session A — Hip strength + quad isometrics** (about 50 min) |
| Tue | Off-day menu: easy (bike ≤45 min Z1–2 or walk ≤40 min on varied ground) + 10 min mobility |
| Wed | **Session B — Single-leg control + eccentric quad** (about 50 min) |
| Thu | Off-day menu (lightest day: mobility, scar work, swim/SUP fine) |
| Fri | **Session C — Multi-planar, athletic prep + low-level plyos** (about 45–50 min) |
| Sat | Free choice from the "well-tolerated" list (SUP, surf, swim, trail hike ≤8 km / ≤150 m descent, easy ride). Later this becomes the **padel slot** |
| Sun | Rest or off-day menu |

- At least 48 h between sessions. Once padel starts it goes on Sat, Session C moves to Thu, and Fri becomes an off-day.
- A long hike or long ride counts as a loaded day. Never put one the day before a session or the day after padel.
- Surf weeks (several surf sessions): drop Session C, keep A and B, and halve the plyos.

## 3. Sessions

General rules for every session:
- **RPE 6–7/10** for effort (2–4 reps left in reserve).
- During the session, knee symptoms up to 2/10 are fine. If one exercise pushes past 3/10, swap it for that exercise's listed regression rather than pushing through.
- Rest 60–90 s for strength, 30–45 s for hip activation, and 2–3 min for isometrics and plyos.
- "Weaker side first, match on the other side": **R first** for hip abductor/rotator work, **L first** for single-leg quad and balance work.

### Warm-up (identical for A/B/C, 8–10 min)
1. Road bike on trainer or outside, **5 min easy**, cadence 85–95, conversational pace. If no bike is available, march or skip lightly in place.
2. 90/90 hip switches × 8 (each side), cat-cow × 8, half-kneeling hip-flexor rock-back with glute squeeze × 8 each side (L first: front-of-hip tightness).
3. Mini-band glute bridge × 12, pause 2 s at top (feet far enough out that the shins are vertical or slightly forward of the knees, as found).
4. Bodyweight sit-to-box (yoga block on a chair or low step) × 8, smooth and relaxed.

### Session A — Hip strength + quad isometrics (about 50 min)

| # | Exercise | Dose | Tempo | Cues / side |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Side-lying hip abduction**, top leg slightly behind body, toes slightly down (glute med bias); add 6 kg mini-band in Phase 2 | 3 × 12–15 | 2-2-2 (2 s up, 2 s hold, 2 s down) | **R first**. No rolling back. Stop the set when you feel it in the front of the hip (TFL) rather than the side. |
| A2 | **Side plank with top-leg abduction** (from knees → feet in Phase 2) | 3 × 20–40 s per side | hold | **R first**, both sides the same time |
| A3 | **Single-leg glute bridge** (other knee hugged to chest) | 3 × 8–12 per side | 1 s up, 2 s hold, 3 s down | **R first**, feet a little further out, push through the heel. Regression: two-leg bridge with mini-band. |
| A4 | **Kettlebell RDL** (8 kg, two-leg → Phase 2 single-leg with fingertip support) | 3 × 10–12 | 3 s down, 1 s up | Soft knees, hips back, long spine. Single-leg version: **R first**. |
| A5 | **Spanish squat isometric**: 15 kg long band looped behind the knees, anchored low to something solid (door anchor or a post), sit back to about 60° knee flexion, shins vertical | 5 × 30–45 s, 2 min rest | hold | Main quad load in Phase 1. Should feel like strong quad work with knee symptoms ≤2. Regression: wall sit at 30–45° of knee bend. |
| A6 | **Calf raise**, two-leg on step edge (Phase 1) → single-leg (Phase 2) | 3 × 12–15 | 2-1-3 (2 s up, 1 s hold, 3 s down) | Single-leg version: **L first** |
| A7 | **Dead bug** with 15 kg band pull-apart overhead or bodyweight | 2 × 8 per side | slow | Lumbar control for the back stiffness |

### Session B — Single-leg control + eccentric quad (about 50 min)

| # | Exercise | Dose | Tempo | Cues / side |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | **Banded lateral walk / monster walk**, mini-band above knees, quarter-squat | 3 × 10 steps each way | controlled | Lead with the right. Toes forward, knees stay over the 2nd toe without forcing them out. |
| B2 | **Standing hip hike (pelvic drop)** on the yoga block, standing leg straight-soft | 3 × 12 | 1-2-2 (1 s up, 2 s hold, 2 s down) | **R stance first**. Isolates glute med in stance, which is what daily standing demands. |
| B3 | **Forward step-down (eccentric)** from yoga block/low step, heel tap to floor | 3 × 8–10 per side | **3–4 s down**, 1 s up | **L first**. Use a mirror: knee over the 2nd toe, pelvis level, no hip drop. Regression: fingertip support on a wall, lower box. Painless clicks are OK. |
| B4 | **Split squat** (8 kg KB goblet in Phase 2), rear foot on floor | 3 × 8–10 per side | 3 s down, 1 s pause, up | **L first**. Range is 0–70° knee bend in Phase 1, deeper in Phase 2. Front shin can move forward. |
| B5 | **Single-leg balance**: eyes open → eyes closed → on folded towel; add 15 kg band perturbations (tied at hip level, face 4 directions) | 3 × 30 s per side | — | **L first**, soft knee about 15–20°. Never stand locked. |
| B6 | **Slant-board (wedge) squat**, two-leg, bodyweight, **partial range** (to about 45–60°) | **Phase 2 only.** Start at 2 × 8 and add 1 set at a time | 3-1-1 (3 s down, 1 s pause, 1 s up) | Treat this as heavy work: **only in B**, never on the same day as another quad-dominant volume increase. Phase 1 version: 2 × 10 heel-elevated (small wedge) goblet squats. |
| B7 | **Banded terminal knee extension** (15 kg band behind knee, anchored) | 2 × 15 per side | 1-2-2 (1 s straighten, 2 s hold, 2 s return) | **L first**. Stop at **soft-straight** without snapping back into hyperextension. |

### Session C — Multi-planar / athletic prep (about 45–50 min)

| # | Exercise | Dose | Tempo | Cues / side |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Clamshell → banded fire hydrant** superset | 2 × 15 + 10 | 2-1-2 (2 s out, 1 s hold, 2 s back) | **R first** |
| C2 | **Lateral lunge** (cossack-lite), bodyweight → 8 kg goblet | 3 × 8 per side | 3 s down, push back | **L first**. The moving knee can travel forward. Prepares for padel lateral lunges. |
| C3 | **Reverse lunge to knee drive** | 3 × 8 per side | controlled down, fast up | **L first**. Smooth, not braced. |
| C4 | **Kettlebell swing** (8 kg, hip hinge) | 4 × 15 | explosive hips | Posterior chain power with low knee load |
| C5 | **Skater stick** (lateral hop → land and hold 2 s), starts as a step, then a small hop | 3 × 6 per side | land quietly | **Phase 2 late / Phase 3.** Knee over toe on landing. Phase 1 version: lateral step and stick. |
| C6 | **Pogo hops** on carpet or mat (**not hard floor**) | Phase 1: 3 × 10 · Phase 2: 3 × 20 · Phase 3: add single-leg 3 × 10 | stiff ankles, soft knees | Low dose, count the contacts. If a delayed reaction appears on day 2–3, halve the dose. |
| C7 | **Copenhagen plank** (short lever, knee on chair) | 2 × 15–25 s per side | hold | Adductors for frontal-plane control |
| C8 | **Tibialis raise** (back on wall) | 2 × 15–20 | 1-1-2 (1 s up, 1 s hold, 2 s down) | — |

### Cooldown (all sessions, 5–8 min)
- Easy bike spin for 3 min, **or** walk.
- Quad/hip-flexor stretch in half-kneeling (L and R, 45 s each), calf stretch on wedge (45 s each), figure-4 glute stretch (45 s each).
- **Femoral nerve / slump slider** (glide, not tension), 10 slow reps per side. Stop well short of any tingling.
- Log the session score (see section 5).

## 4. Off-day menu (choose 1–2 items, about 20–40 min total)

**Active recovery / easy cardio**
- Road bike: Z1–2 (conversational), **cadence 85–100**, ≤45 min on off-days, ≤90 min on the free weekend day. Mostly flat or rolling, no big gear grinding, no intervals. **Never ride hard on legs that are sore from yesterday.** If the quads are sore (DOMS ≥4/10), do an easy spin of ≤30 min or skip the ride. Have the saddle height checked (knee about 25–35° bent at the bottom of the stroke).
- Walking: ≤40 min, preferably on varied/soft terrain, in supportive shoes, with relaxed arms and soft knees. Avoid long city pavement and long standing (stack these like loaded days).
- Swimming, SUP, surfing: green-lit, but count a 2 h+ surf session as a moderately loaded day.

**Mobility (10–15 min)**
- 90/90 hips, couch stretch L>R (front of hip), deep squat hold supported on a doorframe (only in a pain-free range, ≤60 s), thoracic openers, calf on wedge.
- Yoga flow (well tolerated), without hyperextended standing poses: micro-bend the knees in warrior, triangle and so on.

**Soft-tissue / scar (5–10 min)**
- **Left TTT scar mobilization**: skin rolling, then gentle cross-friction and "pin and stretch" with the knee bending, 3–5 min. Then **desensitization**: stroke the scar and the surrounding skin with soft fabric, then a towel, then a rougher fabric, 1–2 min each, a few times a week.
- Patellar mobility: gentle medial/lateral/superior/inferior glides with the knee relaxed and straight (about 30 s per direction). Gentle on the lateral glide for the left knee (instability history).
- Foam roller/ball: lateral quad and glute/TFL for 60 s each, moderate pressure. **Do not** grind the ITB itself.

**Nerve / restless-legs support**
- Slump slider and femoral nerve slider × 10 per side, at night if restless legs are bothering him.
- Keep up the magnesium if it helps. See section 1 about bloodwork.

**Daily posture habits**
- **Stand evenly on both legs with soft knees.** Set a trigger (phone, queue, kitchen counter) to shift weight and unlock the knees. Stop resting idle on the right leg.
- Get up and move every 30–45 min when sitting (sitting makes front-of-hip tightness and buzzing worse). Change position freely.
- Footwear: cushioned, supportive shoes for city days. Use orthoses or taping only short-term if they clearly help.
- Stairs: whole foot on the step, push through the hip, knee over the 2nd toe.

## 5. Progression and regression rules (pain-monitoring model)

**One score: "How did my knees respond?" 0–10** (any knee symptom: pain, buzzing, ache, swelling feeling). Log it:
- (a) right after the session, (b) the next morning, (c) on days 2 and 3 for the delayed reaction.
- Also log a short daily-life baseline on non-session days (also 0–10).

| Zone | Criteria | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Green** | All scores ≤2, back to baseline by the next morning, and no rise on days 2–3 | Progress **one** variable in **one** exercise at the next session of the same type |
| **Amber** | Score 3–4, **or** a rise of ≥2 above baseline on day 2–3, **or** not back to baseline within 24 h | Hold. Repeat the same dose next time. If amber happens twice in a row, regress the last progression |
| **Red** | Score ≥5, **or** swelling, giving way or effusion, **or** symptoms still above baseline at 72 h | Drop the last progression and cut volume by 30–50% for the next 2 sessions. Replace the trigger exercise with its regression. If red happens twice in two weeks, go back a phase |

**Progression order** (one at a time, about every 1–2 weeks per exercise):
1. reps (to the top of the range) → 2. sets (+1) → 3. range of motion → 4. load (8 kg KB, band) / lever (single-leg) → 5. speed/impact.
- **Never** increase more than **two exercises per week**, and never two quad-dominant exercises in the same week.
- Muscle soreness (DOMS) is not a reason to regress. But if DOMS is ≥6/10, the next session should be a lighter one (e.g. swap B with A).
- **Re-entry after ≥7 days off:** week 1 at 60% of the previous sets, week 2 at 80%, then resume.

## 6. Phases and milestones

### Phase 1 — Settle + rebuild base (weeks 1–3)
- Hip work at full volume, quad work isometric/mid-range (Spanish squat, step-down from a low box, split squat 0–70°), no wedge squats, pogo at 3 × 10.
- **Exit criteria:** daily life ≤1/10 most days, all session scores green for 2 weeks, 10 L forward step-downs from 15 cm with good alignment, single-leg balance 30 s each side with eyes open, side plank from knees 40 s each side.

### Phase 2 — Capacity (weeks 4–9)
- Single-leg RDL, single-leg calf raise (target 25 reps each side), goblet split squats, partial-range wedge squats, side plank on feet, skater stick as a step.
- **Exit criteria:** single-leg calf raise ≥25 each side, step-down from 20 cm × 15 each side without valgus or hip drop, single-leg squat to box (about 60°) × 10 each side, L/R difference in reps ≤10%, side plank on feet 45 s each side, 3 weeks without amber/red.

### Phase 3 — Power, multi-planar, padel prep (weeks 10–14)
- Session C gets **padel drills** (indoors on carpet/mat, or on a grass field): split-step → lateral shuffle 3 steps → stick, crossover step and stop, lunge reach to the forehand/backhand corner, 5 × 20 s × 2.
- Lateral bounds, single-leg pogos 3 × 10, single-leg hop and stick.
- **Simple hop self-tests** (with tape on the floor): single-leg hop for distance, triple hop, side hop over a 30 cm line for 30 s. Limb symmetry (L vs R) ≥90%.

### Padel reintroduction — criteria (all must be met)
1. Daily life 0–1/10 for **3+ weeks**.
2. **4 consecutive weeks** of green scores including days 2–3 (no delayed reactions), with Phase 3 drills included.
3. Phase 2 exit criteria still met, plus hop tests ≥90% LSI and 3 × 20 two-leg pogos and 2 × 10 single-leg pogos (each side) on a hard floor with no reaction.
4. 30 min of padel drills (shuffle/split-step/lunge) at home or on a field with no reaction on days 2–3.

**Padel ladder** (one step per week, only after a green week. Drop back a step after amber or red):
1. 30 min of technical rallying (no points), with a 10 min warm-up including the shuffle drills.
2. 45 min of rallying plus a few games.
3. 60 min including a match.
4. 60–90 min match play.
- Day before padel: off-day or easy. Day after: off-day menu only. The next strength session must be at least 48 h after padel.
- Use padel-specific shoes (clay/sand-court soles) for grip and cushioning.

### Running reintroduction — criteria (after padel ladder step 3 is stable for 3+ weeks)
- 30 min of brisk walking on pavement without delayed reaction, single-leg calf raise ≥25 each side, single-leg hop × 10 each side with no reaction, hop tests ≥90% LSI.
- **Walk/run program** on soft surfaces (trail, grass, track): 1 min run / 2 min walk × 8, three times a week max and never the day after padel or a strength session. Add 1 run minute per interval per week if green. Aim for 20 min continuous before adding pavement. Cadence of about 170–180, short steps, easy pace.

### Long term (maintenance)
- Two full strength sessions a week permanently (A+B, or a merged A/B), plus padel, plus whatever sport he chooses. Retest the hop and step-down tests each quarter.

## 7. What I would NOT do (and why)

- **No hard terminal-extension lockouts or locked-knee standing.** These feed hyperextension and fat pad irritation, and they are already a habit on the right side.
- **No wedge squats on more than one day a week, and no high reps, deep range or squats to failure.** He has already shown an extreme quad reaction to them. They are a strong stimulus, so dose them like heavy squatting.
- **No "VMO isolation" focus.** The VMO can't be selectively strengthened in a meaningful way. Whole-quad strength plus hip and trunk control is the evidence-based approach.
- **No stacking loaded days** (padel + hike + hard ride in a row) and **no hard cycling on already-sore legs.** The delayed response makes stacking invisible until it's too late.
- **No progressing several exercises at once.** That already produced systemic fatigue with no clear information about what caused it.
- **No guarded or braced movement cues.** Relaxed, confident movement has been better tolerated.
- **No running or padel matches before the criteria are met**, and no plyos on hard floors before Phase 3.
- **No aggressive ITB foam rolling or aggressive lateral patellar mobilization on the left knee.** Neither helps tracking, and the lateral glide risks irritating an instability history.
- **No long-term reliance on taping, braces or passive treatment.** These are fine as short-term tools for flares or new exposures (e.g. first padel sessions), but not as a permanent solution.
- **No pushing nerve stretches into tingling.** Nerve sliders should be gentle glides, not tension holds.
- **I would not ignore the neural/restless-legs pattern.** A one-off medical screen (lumbar/neural exam, ferritin/B12) is cheap and could change management.
