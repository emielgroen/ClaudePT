Your plan vs. a blind second opinion

A separate agent designed a program from your history, equipment, time budget and a summary of how you've reacted to things. It never saw your current plan or schedule. Below: where it agrees with your plan, where it differs, and my take on each. Nothing changes in your plan until you say so.

Where both agree

  • Hip and quad work together in every session, with hip work placed before the knee work it supports.
  • Wedge squats are a heavy stimulus and need their own small dose.
  • Progress in small steps. Raising several exercises at once caused the "workouts feel heavy" drag.
  • Space loaded days 48–72h apart because of the delayed reaction. No hard cycling on sore legs.
  • Soft knees and even weight on both legs in daily life. Supportive footwear. Break up sitting.
  • Padel and running stay gated until capacity is there. Taping isn't a long-term tool.

The proposed week

Three different sessions instead of one repeated template. The daily page would schedule them dynamically, keeping this order and 48h+ spacing.

AHip strength + quad isometrics · side-lying abduction, side plank, single-leg bridge, 8 kg KB deadlift, Spanish squat holds (15 kg band), calf raises, dead bug

Where they differ

1. Check the nerve symptoms

Adopt
Current plan

Tingling is treated as part of the knee picture (PFPS, scar). Magnesium for restless legs.

Review

Tingling that spreads to the quads and calves, plus restless legs, suggests a nerve part. A one-off GP check: lower back/nerve exam, and bloods for ferritin, B12 and vitamin D.

My take: This is the review's most valuable point. It's a cheap, non-invasive check that could explain the restless legs, since low iron is a common cause. The training itself doesn't change.

2. Three different sessions

Adopt
Current plan

One template repeated 3×/week, with step-ups and step-downs alternating. The 8 kg kettlebell isn't used.

Review

A/B/C sessions, each with a different emphasis. Uses the kettlebell. Hip volume is about equal to quad volume.

My take: This fits your 45+ min budget and fixes the quad-heavy imbalance you noticed on 10/12 and 10/14. More variety also matches your log: varied, multi-planar movement is what your knees tolerate best.

3. Main quad exercise

Adopt
Current plan

Wedge VMO squats are the main quad exercise every session, building toward 3×10 with tempo and lockout squeeze.

Review

Phase 1: Spanish squat holds with the 15 kg band, plus slow step-downs and split squats. Wedge squats only once a week from Phase 2, partial range.

My take: Your log backs this. Even a half session with wedge squats gave "extreme" quad soreness. Isometric holds load the quads hard with little knee irritation.

4. Lockout squeeze and VMO focus

Discuss
Current plan

Built around VMO activation: a hard quad squeeze at lockout on squats, and TKEs to full straight.

Review

The VMO can't really be trained in isolation. Drop hard lockouts and finish at soft-straight instead, because hyperextension feeds right-knee overload and fat pad irritation.

My take: The review fits your own diagnosis framework (mechanisms 2 and 3). But you tolerated the squeeze well on 9/12. Fair test: finish everything at soft-straight for 3 weeks and compare your scores.
