## Current Phase: Phase 1 (PT-Prescribed Strength & Neuromuscular Rehab)

All strength and activation exercises should be performed at the gym (or with appropriate resistance bands/equipment) **3 sets x 10–15 repetitions**, unless specified otherwise. Focus on control, alignment, and specific quad engagement cues.

## Session Structure (Hip/Quad Supersets) — *adopted 2026-09-12*

> The exercises below are grouped into blocks rather than run straight down the list. This changes
> **order only — no exercise, dose or progression is altered by it.** Sections 0–4 remain the
> reference for what each exercise is and how to perform it.

**Block A — Prime** *(low fatigue, always first)*
1. **Z1** Tibialis Raise
2. **Z2** KOT Calf Raise
3. **Glute Bridges**

**Block B — Superset × 3 rounds**
*   **B** Banded Crab Walks ↔ **C** Poliquin Step-Downs

**Block C — Superset × 3 rounds**
*   **A** Clamshells ↔ **D** Heel-Elevated VMO Squats

**Block D — Finish**
*   **3B** L-Sit Level 1 (while the experiment runs)
*   Section 4 cooldown: Figure-4, half-kneeling hip flexor

*(**E** Lateral Step-Ups swaps in for **C** on alternating sessions — see the alternation note in Section 2.)*

### Why this order

1.  **Hip immediately before knee, not hip-block then knee-block.** Pairing a hip exercise directly before a knee exercise means the glutes are **freshly activated while the knee is loaded** — which is the entire mechanism the hip work exists for (controlling femoral rotation so the patella tracks properly, mechanism 1 in `00_Master_Context.MD`). Running all hip work first lets that activation decay before the knee loading it was meant to support.
2.  **Longer local recovery at zero time cost.** Previously A→B ran two glute-medius exercises back-to-back, and C→D ran two quad/VMO exercises back-to-back. Alternating tissue gives each group a full exercise's worth of rest between its own sets — relevant to the systemic fatigue/energy drain reported on 2026-09-12, and to form quality on single-leg work where knee tracking matters most.
3.  **Block A stays fixed at the front.** Z1/Z2 implement the ground-up sequencing (ankle → lower leg → knee → hip). Glute bridges must come before everything else for the reciprocal-inhibition effect they were added for on 2026-09-09 — that only works if the glutes fire *first*.
4.  **Splits the glute-medius double-dip** — clamshells and crab walks now sit in different blocks.

> **⚠️ Execution caveat:** the benefit is **local muscle rest, not reduced total work**. Take a genuine
> breath between the two exercises in a superset. Compressing a superset into a continuous circuit
> turns this into conditioning, and systemic fatigue will break form on the step-downs — precisely
> where knee tracking matters most.

### 0. Ground-Up Warm-Up (Ankle & Lower Leg) — *suggested, not PT-prescribed*

*Added 2026-09-12 from the Knees Over Toes "Knee Ability Zero" program (see reference section at the
bottom of this file). Rationale: the plan previously trained nothing below the knee. Both exercises
place **zero patellofemoral load** and take ~3 minutes combined. Do these **first**, before Section 1 —
the ground-up sequencing (ankle → lower leg → knee → hip) is the core organising principle of that program.*

*   **Exercise Z1: Tibialis Raise (Bodyweight, Wall-Supported)**
    *   **Dose:** 25 reps — 2-second hold at the top, 2-second hold at the bottom.
    *   **Clinical Rationale (Heel-Strike Deceleration):** The tibialis anterior decelerates the foot on every heel strike. Whatever force it fails to absorb travels up to the knee. Directly relevant to Emiel's two top triggers in `00_Master_Context.MD` — **hard/flat surface walking and prolonged standing** — and to mechanism 3 (infrapatellar fat pad sensitivity to repetitive heel strike).
    *   **Execution Cues:** Butt against a wall, feet out a comfortable distance, **legs straight — knees do not bend**. Flex toes up, hold 2 sec, lower under control, hold 2 sec. Scale difficulty by foot distance from the wall: **closer = easier, farther = harder**. Higher-heeled shoes make it harder; flat shoes make it easier. Adjust mid-set as the muscle burns out.

*   **Exercise Z2: KOT (Knees Over Toes) Calf Raise (Bodyweight, Wall-Supported)**
    *   **Dose:** 25 reps. Long-term goal is 25 single-leg reps per side.
    *   **Clinical Rationale (Soleus + Graded Isometric Quad Hold):** Chosen deliberately over the program's FHL Calf Raise. Bending the knee slackens the gastrocnemius and shifts work to the **soleus**, which is more tied to Achilles health and relevant to Emiel's calf-radiating symptoms. Critically, the knee holds a static bent position throughout — making this a **graded isometric quad contraction at a self-selected depth**, and `00_Master_Context.MD` lists *"Isometric Loading & Soft Surfaces"* as a **confirmed relief factor**.
    *   **Execution Cues:** Stand about an arm's length from the wall, hands on wall. Reach knees forward over the toes to a **pain-free** level, then hold that knee angle completely still. Knees, hips and shoulders stay in a straight line. Only the ankles move — flex up, lower down. **Depth is fully self-dosed**: start shallow. Per the program's "strength radiates 15°" principle, even a minimal knee bend builds capacity, so there is never a need to chase depth.

### 1. Warm-Up & Neuromuscular Activation (Hip & Quad Foundation)
*   **Exercise A: Side-Lying Clamshells (Bodyweight Only / 0 kg)**
    *   **Setup:** Lie on your **yoga mat** on your side to cushion the hip joint.
    *   **Dose:** 3 sets x 12 reps per side (2-second hold at the top). *(Progressed 10→12 reps on 2026-09-08, 2→3 sets on 2026-09-12.)*
    *   **Clinical Rationale (Hip Steering Wheel):** Directly targets the *Gluteus Medius* and deep hip external rotators. Strengthening these prevents internal femoral rotation (the thigh rolling inward), which causes the kneecap to rub laterally against the femoral groove (PFPS).
    *   **Execution Cues:** Hips at 45°, knees at 90°. Stack your pelvis and roll your top hip slightly *forward*. Keep heels glued; lift top knee solely using your upper-outer glute. Do not let your lower back or pelvis rotate backward.

*   **Exercise B: Banded Lateral Crab Walks (6kg Decathlon Mini-Band)**
    *   **Dose:** 3 sets x 10 steps left, 10 steps right. *(Progressed 3x6 → 3x8 on 2026-09-02, 3x8 → 3x10 on 2026-09-12 — hip focus is the priority as load increases.)*
    *   **Clinical Rationale (Dynamic Pelvic Endurance):** Trains the endurance of the hip abductors under standing, soft-knee conditions—building the fatigue resistance needed for long standing and walking without triggering lower back compensation.
    *   **Execution Cues:** Place your 6 kg band just above the knees. Set a slight hip hinge with soft knees. Keep feet parallel (toes pointing dead straight ahead). Push knees out against the band as you step sideways, never letting the knees cave inward.

*   **Suggested Addition (not PT-prescribed — added 2026-09-09, do before Exercises A/B):** Glute Bridges (Bodyweight)
    *   **Dose:** 2 sets x 12 reps, 2-second squeeze at the top.
    *   **Rationale:** Added after anterior left hip pain (pinch-quality) surfaced on 2026-09-09. Firing the glutes first (reciprocal inhibition) reduces reliance on the hip flexors, which have likely been overworking since correcting soft-knee standing posture shifted load to the hips (per the 2026-09-02 entry). Chosen deliberately over deeper hip flexor stretching, since a joint-level pinch (vs. a muscular ache) can mean anterior hip impingement — driving into a deep static stretch may aggravate rather than help, whereas bridges unload the hip flexor without putting the joint into deep flexion.
    *   **Execution Cues:** Lie on your back on the yoga mat, feet flat, knees bent. Drive through your heels to lift hips, squeezing glutes hard at the top. Keep ribs down — don't arch the lower back to gain height.

### 2. Main Quad & Knee Strength Circuit (Bodyweight & Home Gear)

> **Session alternation (added 2026-09-12, suggested):** Exercises **C (Poliquin Step-Downs)** and
> **E (Lateral Step-Ups)** now **alternate** — do one per session, not both every session. Both are
> single-leg step movements with a 3-second eccentric and overlap substantially. This halves per-session
> single-leg eccentric volume, which is the lever for the systemic fatigue/energy drain Emiel reported
> on 2026-09-12, and frees the ~5 minutes that Section 0 costs. Each exercise still gets trained
> roughly every other session; progress each one against *its own* last outing, not against the
> calendar.
*   **Exercise C: Poliquin Step-Downs (Bodyweight on Yoga Block or Bottom Stair)**
    *   **Dose:** 3 sets x 8 reps per leg (slow and controlled). *(Progressed 2x6 → 2x8 on 2026-09-08, 2→3 sets on 2026-09-12.)*
    *   **Clinical Rationale (VMO Terminal Lockout & Tendon Capacity):** Replaces machine TKEs. By elevating the heel and letting the working knee travel forward over the toes, this isolates the *Vastus Medialis Oblique (VMO)* in terminal knee extension while safely loading the patellar tendon.
    *   **Execution Cues:** Stand on a yoga block or bottom stair with the working leg. Keep torso perfectly upright. Lower the opposite heel straight down toward the floor by pushing the working knee forward over the 2nd toe (heel can lift slightly). Lightly tap the floor with the free heel (no bouncing!) and push back up, squeezing the VMO hard at lockout.

*   **Exercise D: Heel-Elevated VMO Squats (Bodyweight)**
    *   **Dose:** 3 sets x 10 reps, 4-second descent + 2-second bottom pause + a hard 1–2 second quad squeeze/hold at lockout. *(Tempo added 2026-09-09; lockout squeeze added 2026-09-12. Bodyweight only, by preference as of 2026-09-08 — Emiel doesn't always have a backpack/weight on hand, so external loading was dropped rather than relying on inconsistent equipment.)*
    *   **Clinical Rationale (Patellofemoral Tracking & Anterior Unloading):** Elevating heels (on yoga blocks angled against wall or a 2–3 cm book/plank) allows the knees to travel forward without leaning the torso forward, maximizing quad engagement while removing lumbar strain.
    *   **Execution Cues:** Place heels on elevation, feet hip-width. Stay upright. Descend under control (3 seconds down) keeping knees aligned over 2nd toes. Actively push knees outward to prevent valgus collapse.
    *   **Future progression (no added weight needed):** increase reps/sets, slow the descent further (4–5 seconds), add a 2–3 second pause at the bottom, or add a deliberate hard quad squeeze/hold at lockout — all load the VMO harder without depending on having a weight available.

*   **Exercise E: Lateral Box / Stair Step-Ups (Bodyweight)**
    *   **Dose:** 3 sets x 10 reps per leg. *(Progressed 2→3 sets on 2026-09-08, 8→10 reps on 2026-09-12.)*
    *   **Clinical Rationale (Eccentric Single-Leg Deceleration):** Builds single-leg stability and teaches the hip abductors to prevent the pelvis from dropping during descent.
    *   **Execution Cues:** Stand sideways to a bottom stair or sturdy low surface (~15–25 cm). Place working foot on the step. Drive through the heel to stand tall. Lower down very slowly over 3 full seconds without letting the knee collapse inward.

### 3. Plyometrics & Impact Conditioning
*   **Currently paused (as of 2026-09-02):** Holding off on pogohops until Exercises A–E are progressing well without issue.
*   **Exercise F: Double-Leg Pogohops (Bodyweight on Yoga Mat / Carpet)**

    *   **Dose:** 1–2 sets of 20–30 quick continuous hops.
    *   **Clinical Rationale (Elastic Recoil & Tendon Stiffness):** Reconditions the Achilles and patellar tendons to absorb ground impact smoothly, restoring the natural spring mechanism experienced during festival dancing.
    *   **Execution Cues:** Bounce quickly off the balls of your feet with ankles stiff and knees soft. Minimize ground contact time.

### 3B. Active Experiment: Hip Flexor Strength (L-Sit Level 1) — *time-boxed, suggested*

> **Status: running experiment, started 2026-09-12. Review date: 2026-09-26 (2 weeks).**
> This is **not** a permanent fixture — it exists to settle a specific open question and must be
> resolved on the review date, not left running indefinitely.

*   **Dose:** Level 1 only — 60 seconds total, alternating. Seated on the floor, hands down, lift one leg off the ground for a 2-count, switch, repeat for 60 s. **Lean back to reduce difficulty.** Do not progress to Level 2 (butt off floor) or Level 3 (full L-sit) during the experiment window.
*   **The question being tested:** Emiel developed **new anterior left hip pain (pinch-quality) on 2026-09-09**, after ~3 weeks of steadily escalating quad/glute overload. Two competing explanations:
    1.  **Hip flexor under-strength relative to newly-built extensor strength.** This is the Knee Ability Zero prediction — Ben Patrick explicitly warns that strengthening ankles/knees/glutes without matching hip flexor strength produces hip flexor strain. Notably, this model *predicted Emiel's exact symptom before it appeared*. If true → **loading the hip flexor should help**.
    2.  **Anterior hip impingement-type mechanism** (femur contacting socket/labrum edge at flexion). If true → **loading and deep-stretching the hip flexor should aggravate**.
*   **Stop conditions (decide on or before 2026-09-26):**
    *   Pinch **improves or resolves** → hypothesis 1 supported. Keep the L-Sit, consider progressing toward Level 2.
    *   Pinch **worsens, or sharp pain during the hold** → hypothesis 2 supported. **Stop immediately**, remove from the plan, stop loading that tissue, and reduce hip flexor stretch depth further.
    *   **No change** → inconclusive; drop it rather than leaving it running, and revisit only if the pinch escalates.
*   **Note:** In the meantime the **half-kneeling hip flexor stretch stays gentle, not end-range** (see Section 4) — that caution is unchanged until this experiment resolves.

### 4. Post-Session Decompression & Mobility
*   **Figure-4 Glute Stretch:** 1–2 minutes per side on the yoga mat (releases outer glute/piriformis tension).
    *   *Added rationale (2026-09-12):* Knee Ability Zero's final step is a piriformis stretch, with this reasoning — **a tight piriformis keeps the outside of the knee tight, which makes the VMO harder to contract**. That is mechanism 1 in `00_Master_Context.MD` (VMO vs. lateral quad/IT band imbalance) described from the other direction. This stretch is already well-placed; it is doing more work than a generic cooldown stretch. That program uses a heavier dose (2 × 90 s per side) — worth drifting toward the upper end of the 1–2 min range.
*   **Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch (Tuck Pelvis):** 1 minute per side with the back knee comfortably cushioned on the **folded yoga mat** (decompresses the hip flexors and lower back).
    *   **⚠️ Depth caution (since 2026-09-09):** Go only to a **mild pull, never end-range**, and back off immediately if the *pinch* (as opposed to a stretch sensation) appears. Reason: a joint-level pinch can indicate anterior hip impingement, where driving into a deep static stretch aggravates rather than helps. Glute bridges are the primary tool for that problem, not this stretch. Revisit once the Section 3B experiment resolves.

## Tactical Protection: McConnell Taping Protocol

Use this as your **"external armor"** for high-demand days (city trips, festivals, long walking tours, or future padel/running sessions) while your muscular strength builds:

*   **Step 1 (Onderlaag / Skin Protection):** Sit with leg straight and quad completely relaxed. Apply 2–3 overlapping strips of **Sensitive Hechtpleister (of Hypafix)** loosely over the kneecap (0% stretch).
*   **Step 2 (Bovenlaag / Medial Glide):** Anchor **witte Kruidvat sporttape (niet-elastisch)** on the outer (lateral) edge of the kneecap. Gently push the kneecap inward with your thumb/fingers while pulling the tape firmly across toward the inside (medial) of the knee, securing it on the inner skin.
*   **Removal:** Remove at night before sleep; peel gently in the shower or with warm water.

## Active Recovery & Sports Pacing Guidelines

Integrate these activities according to your baseline symptoms. If you are experiencing a flare-up, scale back to the "High Tolerance" list.

*   **High Tolerance (Do anytime, excellent for active recovery):**
    *   **Yoga:** Keep movements controlled; avoid deep hyper-flexion if it pinches the fat pad.
    *   **Supping (Stand Up Paddleboarding):** Great for dynamic stability and quiet core/hip stabilizers.
*   **Conditional (Pace carefully):**
    *   **Rucking:** Keep backpack weight at 6kg max. Do not ruck if the knees are already in a flare-up.
    *   **Cycling (Racebike):** Keep intensity moderate. Aim for **high cadence (85–95 RPM)** in lighter gears to reduce joint compression per stroke. Avoid cycling when legs are heavily pre-fatigued.
*   **High Risk (Avoid or tightly limit during Phase 1):**
    *   Running, Padel, long flat city walking tours, and prolonged standing (unless using McConnell tape + supportive footwear).

## Daily Life & Postural Adjustments

Implement these simple behavioral changes to reduce daily joint friction and nerve compression:

1.  **Standing "Soft Knee" Hack:**
    *   **Action:** When standing idle (cooking, waiting, chatting), maintain a tiny, almost invisible bend in your knees. Engage your glutes and quads slightly instead of locking the joints back.
    *   **Footrest Hack:** If standing at a counter/desk, rest one foot on a thick book or low step to automatically prevent hyperextending the standing leg.
2.  **Footwear Selection:**
    *   **Action:** Avoid completely flat shoes, barefoot walking, or zero-drop shoes on hard surfaces. Opt for shoes with a moderate heel drop or contoured footbed (like Birkenstocks or supportive sneakers) to mechanically prevent hyperextension.
3.  **Sleeping Positioning:**
    *   **Action:** Keep a small pillow under your knees when sleeping on your back, or between your knees when sleeping on your side. This prevents overnight joint hyperextension and calms nerve buzzing.
4.  **Sitting Posture (90° Rule):**
    *   **Action:** Do not tuck your feet backward under your chair. Keep your feet flat or slightly forward, keeping your knees at a 90° angle or wider to minimize patellar pressure.
5.  **Pacing & Stacking (72-Hour Rule):**
    *   **Action:** Space out high-impact activities (long walks, cycling, rucking) by at least 48–72 hours to prevent delayed onset cumulative flare-ups.

## Reference: Knees Over Toes — "Knee Ability Zero" (analysed 2026-09-12)

*Ben Patrick's zero-equipment program, 10 steps, 3×/week for 12 weeks. Emiel shared the full text on
2026-09-12. Kept here so future sessions don't have to re-derive the analysis. **Only Z1, Z2 and the
Section 3B experiment were adopted** — the rest is recorded as reasoning, not as prescription.*

### The program in full

| Step | Exercise | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tibialis Raise | 25 reps (2s top / 2s bottom) |
| 2 | FHL Calf Raise | 25 |
| 2B | Tibialis Raise again | 25 |
| 3 | KOT Calf Raise | 25 |
| 4 | Patrick Step | 25/side (up to 3 sets) |
| 5 | ATG Split Squat | 5×5/side |
| 6 | KOT Squat *(explicitly optional)* | 5×5 |
| 7 | Elephant Walk | 36/side |
| 8 | L-Sit | 60 s |
| 9 | Couch Stretch | 60 s/side |
| 10 | Piriformis Stretch | 2×90 s/side |

Steps 1–4 run back-to-back with **no break**. Core principle: **build from the ground up** — ankle
→ lower leg → knee → hip. 100 reps of ankle work before the knee is touched at all.

### What maps onto exercises already in this plan

*   **Step 4 (Patrick Step) ≈ Exercise C (Poliquin Step-Downs).** Same Charles Poliquin lineage — Ben Patrick was a student of Poliquin's methods. Same knee-over-toe, upright-torso, self-scaled-depth logic. **The KOT "knee" exercise is already in this plan**; the difference is only how depth is dialled (reach distance vs. block height).
*   **Step 9 (Couch Stretch) ≈ Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch.**
*   **Step 10 (Piriformis Stretch) = Figure-4 Glute Stretch.**

### The single most important finding

Step 8's rationale states that strengthening ankles/knees/glutes **without matching hip flexor
strength** produces hip flexor strain from the resulting imbalance. Emiel spent ~3 weeks
progressively overloading quads and glutes and then developed **new anterior hip pain on
2026-09-09** — the exact predicted failure mode. This converges with the independent reading already
logged in `02_Symptom_Log.MD` ("hip flexors picking up work the glutes should be doing") from the
opposite direction. **This is what the Section 3B experiment is testing.**

### Deliberately NOT adopted, and why

*   **Step 5 — ATG Split Squat.** The program's centrepiece; wrong movement at this stage. (a) Deep loaded knee flexion maximises patellofemoral compression, and sustained flexion is Emiel's clearest aggravator (the 2026-09-06 sitting/"theatre sign" finding). (b) **TTT with a post-op re-dislocation** — deep loaded flexion on a medialized tibial tuberosity is a materially different risk profile than a structurally normal knee. (c) The rear-leg/front-leg position is close to what provokes anterior hip impingement, and the pinch is active. Ben himself gates this behind "Patrick Step easy at full ankle bend," which is far from current level. **Revisit as a Phase 2/3 goal.**
*   **Step 6 — KOT Squat.** The author writes "this exercise is not mandatory" three times for safety. Take him at his word.
*   **Step 2 — FHL Calf Raise.** Redundant with Z2 for Emiel's purposes. It targets big-toe / push-off stability, which isn't a reported problem; Z2 targets the soleus and doubles as an isometric quad hold, both of which are.
*   **Step 7 — Elephant Walk.** Genuinely relevant to the secondary lower-back stiffness noted in `00_Master_Context.MD`, but 36 reps/side is a real time cost against a secondary complaint. **Optional future addition** if session time ever frees up.

### Where the program's philosophy conflicts with this plan — plan wins

*   **"PAIN = DESTRUCTION" / never work through any pain.** This is stricter than current evidence for chronic, sensitised presentations, and stricter than what is demonstrably working for Emiel. He trains at ≤3/10 under the traffic-light rule and has progressed steadily. More directly: the **Aug 19–23 festival finding in `02_Symptom_Log.MD` showed hypervigilant guarding made symptoms worse.** Applied literally, Ben's rule would have halted training weeks ago. **Keep the traffic-light system** (🟢 ≤3/10 settling within 24 h; 🟡 4–5/10 or >24 h — cut volume ~30%; 🔴 sharp joint-line pain, swelling, giving way, or still rising at 48 h — stop).
*   **Rhetorical overreach.** The document contains unsupported claims (specific percentage statistics presented without citation, assertions that doctors knowingly mistreat patients for profit, "no study has ever been done" claims). **The exercise selection and ground-up logic are sound; the surrounding certainty is marketing.** Discount accordingly.
*   **Evidence context.** The mainstream base for PFPS supports **hip + knee strengthening over knee-alone** (JOSPT systematic review/meta-analysis, 2018) — which Sections 1–2 already do well. Ankle/tibialis work for PFPS is *mechanistically plausible for Emiel's specific triggers* but is **not** well-evidenced as a primary PFPS driver. Z1/Z2 were adopted because they are near-zero risk and fill an obvious gap in a chain demonstrably sensitive to heel strike — not because they are proven to resolve PFPS.

### Useful principles worth keeping

*   **"Strength radiates 15°"** — training at one joint angle builds capacity roughly 15° beyond it. Practical consequence: **depth is never required**. Emiel can build knee capacity at conservative, non-provocative angles.
*   **Pain-free muscular burn = construction; joint pain = stop.** This distinction is compatible with the traffic-light rule and is a useful in-session heuristic.
*   **Start the weaker side first** on any single-leg work, and only match that side's ability on the stronger side — otherwise imbalance persists. Relevant given the repeatedly-logged **left-leg balance difficulty** on single-leg step work (Thu 8/27 and Wed 9/9).
