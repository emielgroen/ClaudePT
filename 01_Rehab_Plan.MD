# 01 Rehab Plan & Protocol

## Current Phase: Phase 1 (PT-Prescribed Strength & Neuromuscular Rehab)

All strength and activation exercises should be performed at the gym (or with appropriate resistance bands/equipment) **3 sets x 10–15 repetitions**, unless specified otherwise. Focus on control, alignment, and specific quad engagement cues.

### 1. Warm-Up & Neuromuscular Activation (Hip & Quad Foundation)
*   **Exercise A: Side-Lying Clamshells (Bodyweight Only / 0 kg)**
    *   **Setup:** Lie on your **yoga mat** on your side to cushion the hip joint.
    *   **Dose:** 2 sets x 10 reps per side (2-second hold at the top).
    *   **Clinical Rationale (Hip Steering Wheel):** Directly targets the *Gluteus Medius* and deep hip external rotators. Strengthening these prevents internal femoral rotation (the thigh rolling inward), which causes the kneecap to rub laterally against the femoral groove (PFPS).
    *   **Execution Cues:** Hips at 45°, knees at 90°. Stack your pelvis and roll your top hip slightly *forward*. Keep heels glued; lift top knee solely using your upper-outer glute. Do not let your lower back or pelvis rotate backward.

*   **Exercise B: Banded Lateral Crab Walks (6kg Decathlon Mini-Band)**
    *   **Dose:** 3 sets x 8 steps left, 8 steps right. *(Progressed from 3x6 on 2026-09-02 — hip focus is the priority as load increases.)*
    *   **Clinical Rationale (Dynamic Pelvic Endurance):** Trains the endurance of the hip abductors under standing, soft-knee conditions—building the fatigue resistance needed for long standing and walking without triggering lower back compensation.
    *   **Execution Cues:** Place your 6 kg band just above the knees. Set a slight hip hinge with soft knees. Keep feet parallel (toes pointing dead straight ahead). Push knees out against the band as you step sideways, never letting the knees cave inward.

### 2. Main Quad & Knee Strength Circuit (Bodyweight & Home Gear)
*   **Exercise C: Poliquin Step-Downs (Bodyweight on Yoga Block or Bottom Stair)**
    *   **Dose:** 2–3 sets x 8–10 reps per leg (slow and controlled).
    *   **Clinical Rationale (VMO Terminal Lockout & Tendon Capacity):** Replaces machine TKEs. By elevating the heel and letting the working knee travel forward over the toes, this isolates the *Vastus Medialis Oblique (VMO)* in terminal knee extension while safely loading the patellar tendon.
    *   **Execution Cues:** Stand on a yoga block or bottom stair with the working leg. Keep torso perfectly upright. Lower the opposite heel straight down toward the floor by pushing the working knee forward over the 2nd toe (heel can lift slightly). Lightly tap the floor with the free heel (no bouncing!) and push back up, squeezing the VMO hard at lockout.

*   **Exercise D: Heel-Elevated VMO Squats (Bodyweight or 6kg Backpack)**
    *   **Dose:** 3 sets x 10 reps.
    *   **Clinical Rationale (Patellofemoral Tracking & Anterior Unloading):** Elevating heels (on yoga blocks angled against wall or a 2–3 cm book/plank) allows the knees to travel forward without leaning the torso forward, maximizing quad engagement while removing lumbar strain.
    *   **Execution Cues:** Place heels on elevation, feet hip-width. Stay upright. Descend under control (3 seconds down) keeping knees aligned over 2nd toes. Actively push knees outward to prevent valgus collapse.

*   **Exercise E: Lateral Box / Stair Step-Ups (Bodyweight)**
    *   **Dose:** 2–3 sets x 8–10 reps per leg.
    *   **Clinical Rationale (Eccentric Single-Leg Deceleration):** Builds single-leg stability and teaches the hip abductors to prevent the pelvis from dropping during descent.
    *   **Execution Cues:** Stand sideways to a bottom stair or sturdy low surface (~15–25 cm). Place working foot on the step. Drive through the heel to stand tall. Lower down very slowly over 3 full seconds without letting the knee collapse inward.

### 3. Plyometrics & Impact Conditioning
*   **Currently paused (as of 2026-09-02):** Holding off on pogohops until Exercises A–E are progressing well without issue.
*   **Exercise F: Double-Leg Pogohops (Bodyweight on Yoga Mat / Carpet)**
    *   **Setup:** Stand on your **yoga mat** (folded in half if needed) for joint cushioning.
    *   **Dose:** 1–2 sets of 20–30 quick continuous hops.
    *   **Clinical Rationale (Elastic Recoil & Tendon Stiffness):** Reconditions the Achilles and patellar tendons to absorb ground impact smoothly, restoring the natural spring mechanism experienced during festival dancing.
    *   **Execution Cues:** Bounce quickly off the balls of your feet with ankles stiff and knees soft. Minimize ground contact time.

### 4. Post-Session Decompression & Mobility
*   **Figure-4 Glute Stretch:** 1–2 minutes per side on the yoga mat (releases outer glute/piriformis tension).
*   **Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch (Tuck Pelvis):** 1 minute per side with the back knee comfortably cushioned on the **folded yoga mat** (decompresses the hip flexors and lower back).

## Tactical Protection: McConnell Taping Protocol

Use this as your **"external armor"** for high-demand days (city trips, festivals, long walking tours, or future padel/running sessions) while your muscular strength builds:

*   **Step 1 (Onderlaag / Skin Protection):** Sit with leg straight and quad completely relaxed. Apply 2–3 overlapping strips of **Sensitive Hechtpleister (of Hypafix)** loosely over the kneecap (0% stretch).
*   **Step 2 (Bovenlaag / Medial Glide):** Anchor **witte Kruidvat sporttape (niet-elastisch)** on the outer (lateral) edge of the kneecap. Gently push the kneecap inward with your thumb/fingers while pulling the tape firmly across toward the inside (medial) of the knee, securing it on the inner skin.
*   **Removal:** Remove at night before sleep; peel gently in the shower or with warm water.

## Active Recovery & Sports Pacing Guidelines

Integrate these activities according to your baseline symptoms. If you are experiencing a flare-up, scale back to the "High Tolerance" list.

*   **High Tolerance (Do anytime, excellent for active recovery):**
    *   **Yoga:** Keep movements controlled; avoid deep hyper-flexion if it pinches the fat pad.
    *   **Supping (Stand Up Paddleboarding):** Great for dynamic stability and quiet core/hip stabilizers.
*   **Conditional (Pace carefully):**
    *   **Rucking:** Keep backpack weight at 6kg max. Do not ruck if the knees are already in a flare-up.
    *   **Cycling (Racebike):** Keep intensity moderate. Aim for **high cadence (85–95 RPM)** in lighter gears to reduce joint compression per stroke. Avoid cycling when legs are heavily pre-fatigued.
*   **High Risk (Avoid or tightly limit during Phase 1):**
    *   Running, Padel, long flat city walking tours, and prolonged standing (unless using McConnell tape + supportive footwear).

## Daily Life & Postural Adjustments

Implement these simple behavioral changes to reduce daily joint friction and nerve compression:

1.  **Standing "Soft Knee" Hack:**
    *   **Action:** When standing idle (cooking, waiting, chatting), maintain a tiny, almost invisible bend in your knees. Engage your glutes and quads slightly instead of locking the joints back.
    *   **Footrest Hack:** If standing at a counter/desk, rest one foot on a thick book or low step to automatically prevent hyperextending the standing leg.
2.  **Footwear Selection:**
    *   **Action:** Avoid completely flat shoes, barefoot walking, or zero-drop shoes on hard surfaces. Opt for shoes with a moderate heel drop or contoured footbed (like Birkenstocks or supportive sneakers) to mechanically prevent hyperextension.
3.  **Sleeping Positioning:**
    *   **Action:** Keep a small pillow under your knees when sleeping on your back, or between your knees when sleeping on your side. This prevents overnight joint hyperextension and calms nerve buzzing.
4.  **Sitting Posture (90° Rule):**
    *   **Action:** Do not tuck your feet backward under your chair. Keep your feet flat or slightly forward, keeping your knees at a 90° angle or wider to minimize patellar pressure.
5.  **Pacing & Stacking (72-Hour Rule):**
    *   **Action:** Space out high-impact activities (long walks, cycling, rucking) by at least 48–72 hours to prevent delayed onset cumulative flare-ups.
