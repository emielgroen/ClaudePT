## Training Breaks & Holidays — *adopted 2026-09-13*

### A week off does not cost you strength

Measurable strength loss from complete inactivity does not meaningfully begin until roughly **2–3
weeks**. At one week, essentially nothing is lost, and the slow connective-tissue/tendon adaptations
this plan is actually building are more durable still. Expect to feel slightly rusty for a single
session — that is neural re-familiarisation, not lost capacity.

**Therefore: do not reduce doses after a one-week break. Resume at the same load.** Per the
progression rule, simply do not *progress* on the first session back.

### What actually matters during the break

The exercises are not the main variable — **the holiday's own activity profile is.**
`02_Symptom_Log.MD` shows sedentary days scoring *worse* than active ones (e.g. rest day 3/10 vs.
active varied-movement day 0/10). Cross-reference the tolerance lists in `00_Master_Context.MD`:

*   **Varied, dynamic, multi-planar trips** (hiking, SUP, surfing, snowboarding, yoga) — generally protective. May return in better shape than on departure.
*   **City trips / long flat pavement walking / prolonged standing** — a genuine trigger. Use supportive footwear, keep soft knees when standing, and break up long walks. *(Taping dropped 2026-09-27 — see the taping section.)*

**The daily postural habits are the highest-value thing on a break** and cost zero energy: soft knees
when standing idle, supportive footwear (never flat/barefoot on hard surfaces), pillow under or
between the knees, and breaking up prolonged sitting. The log correlates these with symptoms more
consistently than any single exercise does.

### Optional morning micro-dose (~4 minutes)

Entirely optional — its value is habit continuity, not preventing strength loss. In priority order:

1.  **Tibialis Raise — 25 reps.** Best value by far: no equipment, no knee load, ~90 seconds, and *more* relevant than usual if the trip involves extra walking.
2.  **Glute Bridges — 2 × 12.** Two minutes, no equipment, and the primary tool for the anterior hip tightness.
3.  **Figure-4 Glute Stretch — 1 min per side.**

**Deliberately skip step-downs, VMO squats and step-ups.** These are the fatiguing exercises, and
avoiding that energy cost is the point of the break.

### The break is a free diagnostic

A week with no PT exercises is a clean washout period. **Any symptom that persists through it is not
caused by the training.** Particularly informative for the anterior hip tightness (Section 3B): if it
settles during the break and returns on resuming, that supports a training-load cause; if it persists
unchanged, it does not. Report what happens.

### Loading the week before a break

*   **Do not cram extra volume before leaving.** The break *is* a well-timed deload — there is nothing to compensate for, and banking extra work only means departing with sore legs.
*   **No novel stimulus in the final session before travel.** `00_Master_Context.MD` documents a **Day-3 latency window** on flare-ups, so a new overload 48h before departure peaks on travel day or day one of the trip. Make the last session a straight repeat of the previous one.

### 0. Ground-Up Warm-Up (Ankle & Lower Leg) — *suggested, not PT-prescribed*

*Added 2026-09-12 from the Knees Over Toes "Knee Ability Zero" program (see reference section at the
bottom of this file). Rationale: the plan previously trained nothing below the knee. Both exercises
place **zero patellofemoral load** and take ~3 minutes combined. Do these **first**, before Section 1 —
the ground-up sequencing (ankle → lower leg → knee → hip) is the core organising principle of that program.*

*   **Exercise Z1: Tibialis Raise (Bodyweight, Wall-Supported)**
    *   **Dose:** **2 sets x 25 reps** — 2-second hold at the top, 2-second hold at the bottom. *(Raised from 1 set on 2026-09-14, recording what was actually performed. Matches the source protocol, which runs the tibialis raise twice per session — its Steps 1 and 2B.)*
    *   **Clinical Rationale (Heel-Strike Deceleration):** The tibialis anterior decelerates the foot on every heel strike. Whatever force it fails to absorb travels up to the knee. Directly relevant to Emiel's two top triggers in `00_Master_Context.MD` — **hard/flat surface walking and prolonged standing** — and to mechanism 3 (infrapatellar fat pad sensitivity to repetitive heel strike).
    *   **Execution Cues:** Butt against a wall, feet out a comfortable distance, **legs straight — knees do not bend**. Flex toes up, hold 2 sec, lower under control, hold 2 sec. Scale difficulty by foot distance from the wall: **closer = easier, farther = harder**. Higher-heeled shoes make it harder; flat shoes make it easier. Adjust mid-set as the muscle burns out.

*   **Exercise Z2: KOT (Knees Over Toes) Calf Raise (Bodyweight, Wall-Supported)**
    *   **Dose:** **2 sets x 25 reps**. *(Raised from 1 set on 2026-09-14, recording what was actually performed.)* Long-term goal is 25 single-leg reps per side.
    *   **Clinical Rationale (Soleus + Graded Isometric Quad Hold):** Chosen deliberately over the program's FHL Calf Raise. Bending the knee slackens the gastrocnemius and shifts work to the **soleus**, which is more tied to Achilles health and relevant to Emiel's calf-radiating symptoms. Critically, the knee holds a static bent position throughout — making this a **graded isometric quad contraction at a self-selected depth**, and `00_Master_Context.MD` lists *"Isometric Loading & Soft Surfaces"* as a **confirmed relief factor**.
    *   **Execution Cues:** Stand about an arm's length from the wall, hands on wall. Reach knees forward over the toes to a **pain-free** level, then hold that knee angle completely still. Knees, hips and shoulders stay in a straight line. Only the ankles move — flex up, lower down. **Depth is fully self-dosed**: start shallow. Per the program's "strength radiates 15°" principle, even a minimal knee bend builds capacity, so there is never a need to chase depth.

### 1. Warm-Up & Neuromuscular Activation (Hip & Quad Foundation)
*   **Exercise A: Side-Lying Clamshells (Bodyweight Only / 0 kg)**
    *   **Setup:** Lie on your **yoga mat** on your side to cushion the hip joint.
    *   **Dose:** 3 sets x 12 reps per side (2-second hold at the top). *(Progressed 10→12 reps on 2026-09-08, 2→3 sets on 2026-09-12.)*
    *   **Clinical Rationale (Hip Steering Wheel):** Directly targets the *Gluteus Medius* and deep hip external rotators. Strengthening these prevents internal femoral rotation (the thigh rolling inward), which causes the kneecap to rub laterally against the femoral groove (PFPS).
    *   **Execution Cues:** Hips at 45°, knees at 90°. Stack your pelvis and roll your top hip slightly *forward*. Keep heels glued; lift top knee solely using your upper-outer glute. Do not let your lower back or pelvis rotate backward.

*   **Exercise B: Banded Lateral Crab Walks (6kg Decathlon Mini-Band)**
    *   **Dose:** 3 sets x 10 steps left, 10 steps right. *(Progressed 3x6 → 3x8 on 2026-09-02, 3x8 → 3x10 on 2026-09-12 — hip focus is the priority as load increases.)*
    *   **Clinical Rationale (Dynamic Pelvic Endurance):** Trains the endurance of the hip abductors under standing, soft-knee conditions—building the fatigue resistance needed for long standing and walking without triggering lower back compensation.
    *   **Execution Cues:** Place your 6 kg band just above the knees. Set a slight hip hinge with soft knees. Keep feet parallel (toes pointing dead straight ahead). Push knees out against the band as you step sideways, never letting the knees cave inward.

*   **Suggested Addition (not PT-prescribed — added 2026-09-09, do before Exercises A/B):** Glute Bridges (Bodyweight)
    *   **Dose:** **1 set x 8 reps**, 2-second squeeze at the top. *(Reduced from 2x12 on 2026-09-18 — Emiel reports mild left knee discomfort, not pain, during this exercise. He had already been running 1 set without flagging it; the rep count is newly lowered too.)*
    *   **⚠️ Watch this:** knee discomfort during a hip-dominant hinge is unusual and worth understanding rather than just quietly under-dosing around it. Two things to check: (1) **foot placement** — feet too close to the hips increases knee flexion angle and can transmit load through the knee; try feet a little farther out, closer to a ~90° knee angle at the top. (2) The **sustained knee flexion** a bridge holds throughout the rep is mechanistically closer to the sitting/"theatre sign" pattern already logged as a PFPS trigger (mechanism 1) than to any bridge-specific fault — worth tracking whether it tracks with that pattern.
    *   **Rationale:** Added after anterior left hip tightness surfaced on 2026-09-09. Firing the glutes first (reciprocal inhibition) reduces reliance on the hip flexors, which have likely been overworking since correcting soft-knee standing posture shifted load to the hips (per the 2026-09-02 entry). Keep it regardless of how the Section 3B experiment resolves — it is sound hip work on its own merits.
    *   **Execution Cues:** Lie on your back on the yoga mat, feet flat, knees bent. Drive through your heels to lift hips, squeezing glutes hard at the top. Keep ribs down — don't arch the lower back to gain height.

### 4. Post-Session Decompression & Mobility
*   **Figure-4 Glute Stretch:** 1–2 minutes per side on the yoga mat (releases outer glute/piriformis tension).
    *   *Added rationale (2026-09-12):* Knee Ability Zero's final step is a piriformis stretch, with this reasoning — **a tight piriformis keeps the outside of the knee tight, which makes the VMO harder to contract**. That is mechanism 1 in `00_Master_Context.MD` (VMO vs. lateral quad/IT band imbalance) described from the other direction. ## Daily Life & Postural Adjustments

Implement these simple behavioral changes to reduce daily joint friction and nerve compression:

1.  **Standing "Soft Knee" Hack:**
    *   **Action:** When standing idle (cooking, waiting, chatting), maintain a tiny, almost invisible bend in your knees. Engage your glutes and quads slightly instead of locking the joints back.
    *   **Footrest Hack:** If standing at a counter/desk, rest one foot on a thick book or low step to automatically prevent hyperextending the standing leg.
2.  **Footwear Selection:**
    *   **Action:** Avoid completely flat shoes, barefoot walking, or zero-drop shoes on hard surfaces. Opt for shoes with a moderate heel drop or contoured footbed (like Birkenstocks or supportive sneakers) to mechanically prevent hyperextension.
3.  **Sleeping Positioning:**
    *   **Action:** Keep a small pillow under your knees when sleeping on your back, or between your knees when sleeping on your side. This prevents overnight joint hyperextension and calms nerve buzzing.
4.  **Sitting Posture (90° Rule):**
    *   **Action:** Do not tuck your feet backward under your chair. Keep your feet flat or slightly forward, keeping your knees at a 90° angle or wider to minimize patellar pressure.
5.  **Pacing & Stacking (72-Hour Rule):**
    *   **Action:** Space out high-impact activities (long walks, cycling, rucking) by at least 48–72 hours to prevent delayed onset cumulative flare-ups.

### What maps onto exercises already in this plan

*   **Step 4 (Patrick Step) ≈ Exercise C (Poliquin Step-Downs).** Same Charles Poliquin lineage — Ben Patrick was a student of Poliquin's methods. ### The single most important finding

Step 8's rationale states that strengthening ankles/knees/glutes **without matching hip flexor
strength** produces hip flexor strain from the resulting imbalance. Emiel spent ~3 weeks
progressively overloading quads and glutes and then developed **new anterior hip pain on
2026-09-09** — the exact predicted failure mode. This converges with the independent reading already
logged in `02_Symptom_Log.MD` ("hip flexors picking up work the glutes should be doing") from the
opposite direction. **This is what the Section 3B experiment is testing.**

### Deliberately NOT adopted, and why

*   **Step 5 — ATG Split Squat.** The program's centrepiece; wrong movement at this stage. (a) Deep loaded knee flexion maximises patellofemoral compression, and sustained flexion is Emiel's clearest aggravator (the 2026-09-06 sitting/"theatre sign" finding). (b) **TTT with a post-op re-dislocation** — deep loaded flexion on a medialized tibial tuberosity is a materially different risk profile than a structurally normal knee. (c) Ben himself gates this behind "Patrick Step easy at full ankle bend," which is far from current level. **Revisit as a Phase 2/3 goal.**
*   **Step 6 — KOT Squat.** The author writes "this exercise is not mandatory" three times for safety. Take him at his word.
*   **Step 2 — FHL Calf Raise.** Redundant with Z2 for Emiel's purposes. It targets big-toe / push-off stability, which isn't a reported problem; Z2 targets the soleus and doubles as an isometric quad hold, both of which are.
*   **Step 7 — Elephant Walk.** Genuinely relevant to the secondary lower-back stiffness noted in `00_Master_Context.MD`, but 36 reps/side is a real time cost against a secondary complaint. **Optional future addition** if session time ever frees up.

### Where the program's philosophy conflicts with this plan — plan wins

*   **"PAIN = DESTRUCTION" / never work through any pain.** This is stricter than current evidence for chronic, sensitised presentations, and stricter than what is demonstrably working for Emiel. He trains at ≤3/10 under the traffic-light rule and has progressed steadily. More directly: the **Aug 19–23 festival finding in `02_Symptom_Log.MD` showed hypervigilant guarding made symptoms worse.** Applied literally, Ben's rule would have halted training weeks ago. **Keep the traffic-light system** (🟢 ≤3/10 settling within 24 h; 🟡 4–5/10 or >24 h — cut volume ~30%; 🔴 sharp joint-line pain, swelling, giving way, or still rising at 48 h — stop).
*   **Rhetorical overreach.** The document contains unsupported claims (specific percentage statistics presented without citation, assertions that doctors knowingly mistreat patients for profit, "no study has ever been done" claims). **The exercise selection and ground-up logic are sound; the surrounding certainty is marketing.** Discount accordingly.
*   **Evidence context.** The mainstream base for PFPS supports **hip + knee strengthening over knee-alone** (JOSPT systematic review/meta-analysis, 2018) — which Sections 1–2 already do well. Ankle/tibialis work for PFPS is *mechanistically plausible for Emiel's specific triggers* but is **not** well-evidenced as a primary PFPS driver. Z1/Z2 were adopted because they are near-zero risk and fill an obvious gap in a chain demonstrably sensitive to heel strike — not because they are proven to resolve PFPS.

### Useful principles worth keeping

*   **"Strength radiates 15°"** — training at one joint angle builds capacity roughly 15° beyond it. Practical consequence: **depth is never required**. Emiel can build knee capacity at conservative, non-provocative angles.
*   **Pain-free muscular burn = construction; joint pain = stop.** This distinction is compatible with the traffic-light rule and is a useful in-session heuristic.
*   **Start the weaker side first** on any single-leg work, and only match that side's ability on the stronger side — otherwise imbalance persists. Relevant given the repeatedly-logged **left-leg balance difficulty** on single-leg step work (Thu 8/27 and Wed 9/9).
